So, How Much Is EV Road Tax Now?
A 2026 Malaysian Driver’s Honest Take

No boring jargon – just real talk, fresh rates & a live calculator.

Let me be real with you. When I first heard that the EV road tax exemption was ending on 31 December 2025, I felt a tiny pang of disappointment. For the last few years, owning an electric car felt like a cheat code – cheap “fuel”, minimal maintenance, and zero road tax. It was almost too good to be true.

Well, 2026 arrived, and the free ride (literally) is over. But here’s what I’ve learned after digging into the new rules, talking to JPJ officers, and using the trusty calculator on RoadTaxCalculators.my: it’s really not that bad. In fact, for most of us, the new EV road tax is still surprisingly affordable.

So grab a cup of kopi, and let me walk you through everything you need to know about the 2026 EV road tax in Malaysia – no boring jargon, just the stuff that matters.

First, Take a Deep Breath. The Numbers Are Tiny.

I’ll give you the bottom line upfront. If you drive a normal, everyday EV – think BYD Dolphin, Atto 3, or even a Tesla Model 3 – your annual road tax in 2026 will be somewhere between RM40 and RM320. That’s less than what many people spend on a single weekend grocery run.

Compare that to a petrol car of similar size. A 1.5-litre Honda City or Toyota Vios pays about RM90 a year. A 2.0-litre Civic pays around RM280. So for many EVs, you’re still paying roughly the same – sometimes even less – than a petrol car.

The only EVs that see a noticeable jump are the high-performance monsters like the Porsche Taycan or the BMW iX M60, which can push past RM600 a year. But if you can afford a car that costs half a million ringgit, an extra few hundred ringgit in road tax probably won’t ruin your budget.

How the New “kW” System Works (Without the Headache)

Forget engine capacity. The new system uses kilowatts (kW) – basically, how much power your electric motor produces. The more powerful the car, the more you pay. Fair enough, right?

The JPJ has divided EVs into 11 bands. The smallest band (under 50 kW) pays just RM20 a year. That’s for micro EVs like the Wuling Bingo or some imported kei‑cars. Most family EVs fall into the 100–150 kW band (RM140–RM220) or 150–200 kW band (RM260–RM320).

I checked my friend’s BYD Atto 3 – its motor is rated at 150 kW. That puts it right at the border. He’ll pay around RM220 a year. For context, he used to pay zero. Now he’ll pay less than RM20 a month. He laughed and said, “I spend more on nasi lemak.”

Where to Find Your EV’s kW Number (It’s Super Easy)

You don’t need to guess. Look at your vehicle’s registration card (the one from JPJ). There’s a field labelled “Kuasa (kW)” – that’s your magic number. If you’re still shopping for an EV, just Google “[car model] motor power kW” or check the spec sheet. Every manufacturer lists it.

What About Electric Motorcycles? Still Crazy Cheap.

If you ride an electric scooter or motorbike, the new rates are almost a joke – in a good way. Anything under 40 kW (which is basically every electric motorcycle sold in Malaysia) pays just RM2 per year. Yes, two ringgit. That’s less than the price of a roti canai.

Only seriously powerful electric superbikes (think 40 kW and above) pay RM42 a year. So don’t worry – your little Modenas or Benelli electric scooter is still practically free to keep on the road.

Petrol & Diesel Cars: Nothing Changed

This is important. The 2026 rule changes only affect fully electric vehicles. If you drive a petrol or diesel car – including hybrids – your road tax is still based on engine cc, just like before. No surprises.

So if you’re driving a Myvi 1.5L, you’ll still pay around RM90. A Proton X70 1.8L? Still RM280. The calculator on RoadTaxCalculators.my handles both systems side by side.

One More New Thing: Flexible Payment for E‑Hailing Drivers

This doesn’t apply to everyone, but it’s worth mentioning because it’s a smart move. Starting 2026, e‑hailing drivers can now renew their road tax for 1, 2, or 3 months instead of the usual 6 or 12. This helps drivers manage cash flow.

For example, a 1,500cc e‑hailing car can pay just RM8.50 for one month, RM15 for two months, or RM22.50 for three months. That’s a huge help for gig workers. If you’re an e‑hailing driver, check with your app or JPJ for the exact process – but the calculator on this website can show you the pro-rated amounts.

Let’s Bust a Few Myths Right Now

❌ Myth #1: “EV road tax will be as expensive as a big petrol car.”
✅ Nope. Even the most powerful EVs under 300 kW pay less than RM600. A 3.0-litre petrol BMW pays over RM1,500. EVs are still heavily subsidised.

❌ Myth #2: “I have to convert horsepower to kW myself.”
✅ You can, but you don’t need to. The registration card shows kW directly. If you only see PS, multiply PS by 0.7355. Or just use the online calculator – it does the conversion for you.

❌ Myth #3: “The exemption ended, so now EVs are expensive to own.”
✅ Let’s do the math. A typical EV saves you RM1,500–RM3,000 a year in fuel compared to a petrol car. Even if your road tax goes from RM0 to RM200, you’re still way ahead. Plus, EV maintenance is cheaper – no oil changes, fewer brake jobs, no timing belts.

Real Talk: Should You Still Buy an EV in 2026?

Absolutely. The road tax change doesn’t move the needle. The real reasons to buy an EV are still the same: silent driving, instant torque, lower running costs, and a smaller carbon footprint. The new road tax is just a small, predictable annual fee – like paying for parking or tolls.

If you’re on the fence, do this: look up the kW rating of the EV you want, then use the calculator to see the exact road tax. I’ll bet you’ll be surprised how low it is.

What About Renewal? Same Process, Different Amount.

You still renew your EV road tax the same way: through MyJPJ app, at JPJ counters, or at post offices. The only difference is you’ll now see a fee instead of “RM0”. No extra forms, no special inspection – just a normal transaction.

And if you forget to renew? The penalty is the same as for any other vehicle: a late fee plus possible compounding. So set a reminder on your phone.

Now that EVs are everywhere (I see five Teslas on my way to work every day), it’s fair to ask owners to contribute a little to road maintenance and the system.

The good news is that the government designed the new rates to be light and progressive. Most of us will pay less than RM300 a year. That’s less than a cup of Starbucks per week.
